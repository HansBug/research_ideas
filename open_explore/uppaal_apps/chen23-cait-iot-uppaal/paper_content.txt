SUMMARY With the support of emerging technologies such as 5G,
machine learning, edge computing and Industry 4.0, the Internet of Things
(IoT) continues to evolve and promote the construction of future networks.
Existing work on IoT mainly focuses on its practical applications, but there
is little research on modeling the interactions among components in IoT
systems and verifying the correctness of the network deployment. There-
fore, the Calculus of the Internet of Things (CaIT) has previously been
proposed to formally model and reason about IoT systems. In this paper,
the CaIT calculus is extended by introducing broadcast communications.
For modeling convenience, we provide explicit operations to model node
mobility as well as the interactions between sensors (or actuators) with the
environment. To support the use of UPPAAL to verify the temporal prop-
erties of IoT networks described by the CaIT calculus, we establish a rela-
tionship between timed automata and the CaIT calculus. Using UPPAAL,
we verify six temporal properties of a simple “smart home” example, in-
cluding Boiler On Manually, Boiler O ﬀAutomatically, Boiler On Auto-
matically, Lights On, Lights Mutually, and Windows Simultaneously. The
veriﬁcation results show that the “smart home” can work properly.
key words: IoT, CaIT calculus, timed automata, UPPAAL
1. Introduction
The emergence of the Internet of Things (IoT) shifts the de-
velopment focus of the Mobile Internet from networks of
“end users” to networks of “objects”, where “objects” can
autonomously interact for a common goal. The “things”
contained in IoT systems can be RFID tags (identifying
objects), sensors (capturing the changes in the environ-
ment), and actuators (providing information to the environ-
ment). Recently, IoT has been gradually combined with
other technologies such as cloud computing [1], artiﬁcial in-
telligence [2], vehicle networks [3], etc. Relevant research
results have been applied in many ﬁelds, including envi-
ronmental monitoring, health care, smart environment, and

Both model-checking techniques and process algebras
are powerful tools for formal researches. Krishna et al.
adopted Business Process Model Notation (BPMN) to ex-
press the underlying IEC 61499 speciﬁcation [4]and de-
veloped an IoT Composer tool to support the develop-
ment of IoT applications [5]. All of these studies apply
model-checking approaches for veriﬁcation, i.e., systems
are checked against formulas given in temporal logic [6].
Diﬀerently, process algebras focus on taking (higher-level)
system descriptions as speciﬁcations for (more detailed)
system speciﬁcations, which are useful for modeling a pro-
gramming abstraction that concentrates, especially for chal-
lenges in developing distributed and concurrent systems [7].
Both model-checking approaches and process algebras have
their strengths and can be mutually beneﬁcial. Process al-
gebras are more abstract and expressive, which can provide
a complete and accurate formal speciﬁcation of systems [8].
For instance, the equivalence and reﬁnement relations em-
ployed in process algebras are compositional. Hence, one
(smaller, more analyzable) equivalent system can be sub-
stituted for another (larger, more complex) one, improving
the eﬃciency of model-checking [7]. Using model-checking
techniques, especially various tools, it is possible to mecha-
nize the systems described by a process calculus and further
verify their properties. Thus, some studies combine model-
checking techniques with process algebras [9],[10]. Jack-
son translated Timed CSP processes into time automata to
verify the logical properties of CSP processes using timed
automata techniques [9]. Ouaknine et al. have proved
the equivalence between Timed CSP and closed timed au-
tomata [10]. Inspired by these works, we adopt process al-
gebras and model-checking techniques together to explore
IoT systems.
Some works have been done to model and verify IoT
systems from the perspective of process calculi. Lanese
et al. proposed the ﬁrst calculus for IoT systems, called
IoT-calculus, to capture the interactions between sensors,
actuators, and computer processes to provide services for
“end users” [11]. To express a discrete notion of time and
verify the temporal properties of IoT systems, Lanotte et

In recent years, a signiﬁcant amount of work has been
achieved in formal modeling and analyzing broadcast be-
havior from the views of process algebras. The Calculus for
Broadcasting Systems (CBS) [13]is the ﬁrst calculus to take
broadcast as the communication primitive. b-calculus [14]
extends -calculus by introducing a broadcast paradigm.
However, they only focus on global communication, i.e., al-
lowing all nodes to receive broadcast messages at the same
time, which makes the information vulnerable to eavesdrop-
ping by attackers. Thus, we refer to process calculi for local
broadcast (such as CBS #,CWS , and w-calculus) to further
limit the transmission area of broadcast messages [15]–[17].
Consequently, our calculus requires that only adjacent nodes
in IoT systems listening to the corresponding channel can
receive the broadcast.
The CaIT calculus is a fully abstract semantic theory,
proposed to specify and reason on IoT applications [12].
Using the CaIT calculus, various IoT systems can be de-
scribed as similar abstract models, which is much clearer
and more concise than describing them by directly applying
model checking techniques [7],[12]. Furthermore, the CaIT
calculus can support the future exploration of the relation-
ship between IoT systems and has been used to explore the
equivalence between IoT systems [12]. Whereas, analyzing
the relationships between multiple IoT systems directly us-
ing model checking techniques is much more complicated.
However, there are currently no tools that support the veri-
ﬁcation and analysis of IoT systems modeled by the CaIT
calculus. To further use tools to mechanize the IoT sys-
tems modeled by the CaIT calculus and explore their tempo-
ral properties, we establish a relationship between the CaIT
calculus and timed automata. Guided by this relationship,
we construct a series of timed automata for the CaIT cal-
culus, involving basic commands (or statements) and par-
allel compositions. Numerous tools can mechanize these
automata, among which UPPAAL supports an intuitive and
understandable graphical representation and is widely used
to explore channel communications.
The main contributions of this paper include the fol-
lowing three aspects:
The CaIT calculus is extended by applying broadcast
communications as well as adding explicit operations
for describing node mobility and interactions between
sensors (or actuators) with the environment.
We build the relationship between the CaIT calculusand timed automata, involving each basic command (or
statement) and the parallel composition. We implement
all of these automata in UPPAAL, and further explore
the networks of timed automata.
Based on this relationship, an example “smart home”
is modeled. Using UPPAAL, we verify its six temporal
properties.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-
tion 2 introduces the CaIT calculus and presents a simple
“smart home”. Section 3 describes the relationship be-
tween the CaIT calculus and timed automata. Section 4
gives a brief introduction to UPPAAL and the implemen-
tation of the CaIT calculus in UPPAAL, which allows us to
achieve the “smart home” and verify some temporal proper-
ties. Conclusion and future work are given in Sect. 5.

3. From the CaIT Calculus to Timed Automata
CaIT is a timed process calculus with a discrete notion of
time, as it provides a special timing action to synchro-
nize global time, and equips some actions (e.g., communi-
cations) with timeouts to control their execution [12]. Thus,
CaIT can be mapped into timed automata. Inspired by
the work [9],[10],[19]–[21], we explore the transformation
from the CaIT calculus to timed automata, which enables us
to use UPPAAL to verify some properties of IoT networks
described by the CaIT calculus.

guage, a model-checker, and a simulator.
Description Language:
By deﬁning templates with a set of parameters that can
be any type, UPPAAL can model a real-time system as a
network of timed automata. Taking a timed automaton as a
directed graph, locations are its vertices connected by edges.
Locations. There are three types of locations: initial loca-
tions, urgent locations, and committed locations.
(1)Initial Locations: Each template has one and only
one initial location, which is described as a double circle.
(2)Urgent Locations: Urgent locations prohibit time
pass, and an urgent location is described as a U in a circle.
(3)Committed Locations: Similarly, committed loca-
tions also freeze time, and a committed location is repre-
sented as a C in a circle. Once a template reaches a commit-
ted location, then the following transition must have an out-
going edge of at least one of the committed locations [31].
Edge. Edges are noted with four types of labels: selec-
tions, guards, synchronizations, and updates.
(1)Selections: A selection label can bind a speciﬁc
identiﬁer to a random value belonging to a given type.
(2)Guards: A guard expresses conditions on the values
of clocks and integer variables that must be satisﬁed before
the edge can be taken [31].
(3)Synchronizations: The synchronization label is
used to synchronize channel communication, which can be
c! (outputting) or c? (inputting), where cstands for a chan-
nel.
(4)Updates: The update label is a comma-separated
list of expressions, where these expressions only involve
clocks, integer variables, and constants, and just assign inte-
ger values to clocks [22].
Model-checker:
By exploring the whole reachable state space, the
model-checker can verify the reachability, safety, and live-
ness properties of a system. In UPPAAL, these properties
are speciﬁed in a subset of Computational Tree Logic (CTL)
[22].
Simulator:
During the early design (or modeling) stages, the simu-
lator allows users to examine the possible dynamic behavior
of a system in an interactive and graphic style.

5. Conclusion and Future Work
In this paper, we enriched the CaIT calculus by introducing
broadcast communications and adding explicit processes to
model node mobility, the interactions between sensors (or
actuators) with the environment. In order to adopt UPPAAL
to explore the temporal properties of IoT networks described
by the CaIT calculus, we have built a relationship between
the CaIT calculus and timed automata. Then we gave a sim-
ple “smart home” to show the application of this relation-
ship. By applying UPPAAL, we have veriﬁed six temporal
properties of the “smart home”. The veriﬁcation results in-
dicate that the “smart home” can operate properly.
In the future, we will further improve our transforma-
tion performance, make it more automatic, and implement it
using tools based on discrete time models. Further, we will
try to apply it to more complex physical scenes and explore
more interesting properties, such as the equivalence and re-
ﬁnement relations between IoT systems. In addition, many
studies have successfully implemented the conversion be-
tween automata and ﬁxed-point logic [33],[34], which will
enable us to explore ﬁxed-point operators more deeply. Fi-
nally, our calculus will be combined with Quality Calculus
to support communications with quality predicates [35]. As
a result, our calculus can capture features of IoT systems
(e.g., the node mobility, and behaviours of sensors and ac-
tuators), and ensure that components work normally even
when communication becomes unreliable.
