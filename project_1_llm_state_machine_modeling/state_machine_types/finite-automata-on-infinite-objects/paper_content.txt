Introduction
   This thesis studies infinite behaviors of several kinds of finite au-
tomata on infinite objects. Namely, alternating finite automata on co-
words, finite tree automata on infinite trees which move from the root 
infinitely, and finite biautomata on two-sided infinite words are investi-
gated through six types of acceptance conditions. Each condition is an 
appropriate extension of the one used for acceptance of finite automata 
on co-words. 
   An co-word is an infinite sequence of symbols of finite kinds and a 
set consisting of co-words is called an co-language. Originated in Bi chi 
[2] , co-languages have received considerable attentions through various 
infinite computation models [4-10, 12-15, 18, 20-30, 32-34] . In particular, 
nondeterministic and deterministic finite automata on co-words have 
been extensively studied through a variety of acceptances and the corre-
sponding co-language classes have been precisely determined [2, 4, 9, 12, 
15, 18, 20-21, 24, 29, 32-34] . By these studies six types of acceptance 
conditions are established as standard for acceptances of co-words by fi-
nite automata. Moreover, they are shown to be sufficient to classify co-
language classes defined by finite automata [18, 32-34] . It is known, in 
contrast with the case of finite length words, that for two of these six 
types of acceptance conditions nondeterministic finite automata define 
larger classes of co-languages than deterministic ones. The reader is 
suggested to look up the table in [34, p. 129] for details. 
   By the discussion above it is natural to use appropriate extensions 
of these six types of acceptance notions for studying infinite behaviors 
of several kinds of finite automata on infinite objects. The main aim of


--- Page 4 ---
this thesis is to investigate how determinism, nondeterminism or  al-
ternation affects the recognition power of infinite objects, i.e. co-words, 
infinite trees and two-sided infinite words by alternating finite automata, 
finite tree automata and finite biautomata, respectively, under these six 
types of acceptance conditions. 
   Chapter 1 deals with finite automata on co-words. This chapter re-
views known resuts concerning nondeterministic and deterministic finite 
automata on co-words which are relevant to successive chapters. As an 
application of infinite behaviors of finite automata we analyze the lan-
guages described by flow expressions without synchronization mecha-
nisms. The flow expressions, used to describe flows of sequential and 
concurrent processes in software descriptions [31] , are defined as regular 
expressions augmented with operators II (shuffle), t (shuffle closure) and 
co (infinite repetition) . It is shown that the class of languages defined 
by the flow expressions with • , * , U , II and co coincides with that of in-
finitary regular languages [24] . We also show that the shuffle closure 
operator increases the denotational power of flow expressions with the 
infinite repetition operator. 
   Alternating finite automata on co-words are treated in Chapter 2. 
The main part of this chapter is due to the paper [22] . As an extended 
notion of nondeterminism, alternation has been studied in relation to 
the complexity theory [3, 19] . We introduce alternation to finite au-
tomata on co-words. The classes of co-languages defined by alternating 
finite automata are investigated under six types of acceptance conditions. 
Among these six classes we can characterize four classes. For charac-
terization of remaining two classes we only get partial results. There-
fore full characterization of these two classes is an open problem. 
   Chapter 3 is concerned with finite tree automata on infinite trees 
introduced by Rabin [27-29] . The content of this chapter is based on 
[14] . Nondeterministic and deterministic finite tree automata on infinite 
trees which move from the root infinitely are investigated through six 
types of acceptance conditions. We relate the six classes of infinite 
tree languages recognized by nondeterministic (resp. deterministic) finite 
tree automata with the corresponding classes of co-languages defined by 
nondeterministic (resp. deterministic) finite automata. The relationship 
among these six classes is also established.

   Finite biautomata on two-sided infinite words are treated in Chapter 
4. We define a two-sided infinite word as the equivalence class under 
the shift of a biinfinite word (i.e. a mapping from the set of integers 
Z into a finite alphabet  E)  . A finite biautomaton is a pair of finite au-
tomata, one of which runs leftward infinitely while the other runs right-
ward infinitely starting at some point of a two-sided infinite word. The 
notion of finite biautomata was introduced by Nivat and Perrin [25] . 
They defined nondeterministic and deterministic finite biautomata through 
Buchi-type acceptance condition [2] . We consider nondeterministic 
and deterministic finite biautomata on two-sided infinite words under six 
types of acceptance conditions including Biichi's one. The classes of 
two-sided infinite languages defined by finite biautomata are examined 
in this chapter.
Chapter 1. Finite automata on co-words
1.1. Introduction
   The first half of this chapter reviews known results concerning non-
deterministic and deterministic finite automata on co-words which are 
relevant to Chapters 2-4. In Section 1.2 we define six types of accept-
ance conditions for finite automata on co-words. Each condition uses a 
family of final sets for acceptance as was done in [21] . So called Biichi-
type acceptance [2] and Muller-type acceptance [21] are included in 
these six types of acceptances. 
   Characterization of the classes of co-languages defined by nondeter-
ministic and deterministic finite automata under six types of acceptance 
conditions are summarized in Section 1.3. Closure properties of these 
classes are also mentioned. The class of co-languages defined by deter-
ministic (resp. nondeterministic) finite automata under Muller-type 
(resp. Buchi-type) acceptance is called the class of co-regular languages 
[21] . This class is the most important one. 
   As an application of infinite behaviors of finite automata we analyze 
the languages described by flow expressions without synchronization 
mechanisms in Section 1.4. Flow expressions are introduced by Shaw [31] 
to describe flows of sequential and concurrent processes. The flow ex-
pressions are defined as regular expressions augmented with operators 
shuffle, shuffle closure and infinite repetition. The infinite repetition op-


--- Page 6 ---
erator is used to describe inherently nonterminating flows. The  shuffle 
operator describes the concurrent executions of processes. On the other 
hand, the shuffle closure is used to describe a variable number of inter-
leaves of some flow. 
   Formal properties of the languages defined by the flow expressions 
without the infinite repetition operator have been studied in the litera-
tures [1, 11, 16] . If the flow expressions are allowed the synchroniza-
tion mechanisms such as locks and waits/signals, then any recursively enu-
merable set can be described [1, 16] . It is also known that if no syn-
chronization mechanism is allowed, then the languages described by the 
flow expressions without the infinite reperator are context-sensitive [11] . 
   On the other hand, if the infinite repetition operator is allowed, 
then the languages described by such flow expressions may contain both 
finite length words and infinite length words. A language which may 
contain both finite length words and infinite length words is called an 
infinitary language [24] . We say that an infinitary language is regular 
if the part consisting of infinite legth words is co-regular and the remain-
ing part is regular. The class of infinitary regular languages has play-
ed an important role in [24] . We prove that the flow expressions with-
out shuffle closure give the class of infinitary regular languages. It is 
known that the shuffle closure operator increases the denotational power 
of flow expressions if the infinite repetition operator is not allowed. 
We show that the same fact also holds for 0-languages defined by the 
flow expressions with the infinite repetition operator.

Chapter 3. Finite tree automata on infinite trees
3.1. Introduction
   Rabin [27-29] introducedfinite tree automata on infinite trees to 
study the problems of definabillity in monadic second-order theories. As 
by-products, he obtained several decidability resuts. He considered the 
acceptance conditions which are extensions of Cl and C5 types discussed 
in Chapters 1 and 2 to infinite tree cases. In this chapter we deal with 
all six types of acceptance conditions extended for finite tree automata 
on infinite trees which move from the root infinitely, and study the 
classes of infinite tree languages defined by finite tree automata under 
these six types of acceptances. Each condition uses a family of final 
sets for acceptance as was done in Chapters 1 and 2 for finite automata 
on (0-words. The purpose of this chapter is to establish the relationship 
among these infinite tree language classes. First it is proved that a sin-
gle final set is sufficient for four types of acceptance definitions. We 
also prove an embedding theorem which relates the infinite tree language 
classes with the (0-language classes. With the aid of this embedding 
theorem and a result due to Rabin [28] , we classify the infinite tree 
language classes. A summary of the results is figured out in Fig. 3-1 
in Section 3.5. 
   It is known that the nondeterministic infinite tree language class is 
larger than the deterministic one for any type of the acceptances [30] . 
To refine this result, we consider the nondeterministic degree which is 
defined to be the maximum number of possible transitions of a finite 
tree automaton. Then the determinism is regarded as the nondetermin-
istic degree one. We show that the nondeterministic degree gives a 
hierarchy between the nondeterministic class and the deterministic one 
for each acceptance definition. This is a property special to (infinite) 
tree languages. In addition to this observation, the results in this chap-


--- Page 26 ---
ter show that the class of infinite tree languages accepted by nondeter-
ministic finite tree automata which accept in the sense of Muller  (Co 
type acceptance) is the most general one and this class may be consid-
ered as a natural extension of the class of co-regular languages [21] , 
where deterministic finite automata are used as basis.

Chapter 4. Finite biautomata on two-sided infinite words
4.1. Introduction
   We define a two-sided infinite word as the equivalence class under 
the shift of a biinfinite word (i.e. a mapping from the set of integers 
Z into a finite alphabet E). Finite biautomata on this kind of words 
were firstly investigated by Nivat and Perrin [25]. They represent a 
biinfinite word as an element in Ew x Ew. A finite biautomaton is 
defined to be a triple consiting of two finite automata and a subset 
made of pairs whose first (resp. second) component is a state of the 
first (resp. second) finite automata. This set is called an initial set of 
a finite biautomaton. Given a biinfinite word (x, y) in Ew x Ea', one of 
pairs in the initial set is chosen and the first (resp. second) finite autom-
aton runs on x (resp. y) infinitely starting with the first (resp. second) 
component of the pair as its initial state. 
   Nivat and Perrin defined both nondeterministic and deterministic 
finite biautomata through one kind of acceptance condition due to litchi 
[2] and called them bilateral if the biinfinite languages accepted are 
closed under the shift operation. Thus a bilateral finite biautomaton 
becomes an acceptor for two-sided infinite languages. In this chapter 
we study both nondeterministic and deterministic finite biautomata under 
six types of acceptance conditions discussed in Chapters 1-3. A finite 
biautomaton is called strict if its initial set is a singleton. We show 
that strict bilateral finite biautomata are less powerful than bilateral 
finite biautomata under each acceptance condition. We investigate 
interrelationship of classes of two-sided infinite languages defined by 
bilateral finite biautomata. Characterization of several classes of two-
sided infinite languages is also given.
