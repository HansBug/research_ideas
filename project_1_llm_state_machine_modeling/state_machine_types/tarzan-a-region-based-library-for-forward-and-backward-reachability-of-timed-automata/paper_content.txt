Abstract.The zone abstraction, widely adopted for its notable practi-
cal efficiency, is the de facto standard in the verification of Timed Au-
tomata (TA). Nonetheless, region-based abstractions have been shown to
outperform zones in specific subclasses of TA. To complement and sup-
port mature zone-based tools, we introduceTarzan, a C++region-based
verification library for TA. The algorithms implemented inTarzanuse
a novel region abstraction that tracks the order in which clocks become
unbounded. This additional ordering induces a finer partitioning of the
state space, enabling backward algorithms to avoid the combinatorial ex-
plosion associated with enumerating all ordered partitions of unbounded
clocks, when computing immediate delay predecessor regions.
We validateTarzanby comparing forward reachability results against
the state-of-the-art tools Uppaal and TChecker. The experiments con-
firm that zones excel when TA have large constants and strict guards. In
contrast,Tarzanexhibits superior performance on closed TA and TA
with punctual guards. Finally, we demonstrate the efficacy of our back-
ward algorithms, establishing a foundation for region-based analysis in
domains like Timed Games, where backward exploration is essential.
1 Introduction
Formally verifying the behavioral correctness of real-time systems (also including
distributed systems) is essential for ensuring that critical properties hold. Since
their introduction, Timed Automata (TA) have become one of the most widely
adopted formalisms for modeling and analyzing such systems [15,31].
A key challenge in the verification of TA is their infinite state space, addressed
by theregion[1] abstraction, which provides a finite representation enabling
algorithmic verification. Alternatives includeBinary Decision Diagrams[35] and
zones[18], the latter requiringnormalizationfor state space finiteness [9]. Zones,
typically implemented usingDifference Bound Matrices (DBMs)[16], are widely
used in tools such as Uppaal [6], TChecker [19], Kronos [13], and Synthia [28].

Zones have shown to be highly efficient in practice and, over the last decades,
have become the de facto standard for the formal verification of TA, although
they have well-known limitations [11,24,36]. Yet, prior work has demonstrated
that for the class ofclosedTA,i.e., TA containing only non-strict guards, region-
based abstractions may outperform state-of-the-art zone-based tools [21].
The promising performance of region-based abstractions on appropriate sub-
classes of TA motivated the development ofTarzan(Timed Automata Region
and Zone library for real-time systems ANalysis) [26], a C++20 library designed
to support and complement state-of-the-art zone-based tools for the formal ver-
ification of TA. Currently,Tarzansupports only region-based reachability of
TA; optimizations leveraging zones are deferred to future work. The implemen-
tation of backward reachability algorithms enables the extension ofTarzanto
domains in which backward analysis is essential, such as Timed Games [25].
When compared against zone-based state-of-the-art tools including Uppaal
and TChecker,Tarzanexhibits superior performance on TA and networks of TA
featuringpunctualguards,i.e., guards with equality constraints, and on closed
TA, even with large constants or many clocks. However, the size of constants
remains a significant bottleneck in other classes of TA (e.g., zones excel in TA
with strict guards). Moreover, sinceTarzanonly supports reachability analysis,
using forward reachability to verify safety properties for unreachable regions
requires exploring the entire reachable state space. We show in this paper that
backward reachability can enhance the verification of safety properties.
Related worksAbstractions closely related to regions have already been
proposed to better capture the semantics of TA. In [37], regions are encoded as
Boolean formulae via discrete interpretations. Theslot-basedabstraction pro-
posed in [2] groups regions by time using integer-bounded intervals over a global
clock. A data structure, calledtime-dart, is introduced in [21] to compactly
encode configurations of TA and their time successors without explicit enumera-
tion. In [10], regions of bounded TA are represented as tuples combining integer
clock values and a partition of clocks that reflects the ordering of their fractional
parts. The region abstraction is extended in [12] todurational-actionTA.
To the best of our knowledge, no region-based library for TA verification,
nor any libraries or tools based on the aforementioned abstractions, currently
exist. Consequently, we compareTarzanagainst the state-of-the-art tools Up-
paal and TChecker. Existing libraries that explicitly support the analysis of TA
using DBM-based zone representations include [30,33]. Other libraries offer more
general polyhedral abstractions beyond zones, such as those provided by [3,29].
ContributionsThe main contribution of this work is two-fold: (i) we first
introduce a refined region representation that tracks the order in which clocks
become unbounded. While this representation may increase the total number of
regions by inducing a finer partitioning of the state space, a major advantage is
that it guarantees at most three immediate delay predecessors for any region;
(ii) building on this new representation, we develop and implement algorithms in
Tarzanfor computing delay and discrete successors and predecessors of regions.
Existing tools may integrateTarzanto enhance their verification capabilities.

2 Theoretical Background

of TA, which is obtained by extending classical finite-state automata with real-
valued clocks. In the following, we use the term action to refer to the events that
a real-time system can exhibit. TA are defined as follows [1]:

3 Representing Regions with Ordered Clock Partitions
We introduce a new region representation that captures the order in which clocks
become unbounded (exceedingc m). For bounded clocks (with value up toc m),
only the ordering of their fractional part is preserved. Our representation builds
on [10], which focuses on TA with bounded clocks. By also handling unbounded
clocks, we significantly extend the applicability of region-based state space explo-
ration to a broader class of TA. While forward exploration remains unaffected,
our representation influences backward exploration (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2).

Definition 4 (Region class).If the order in which clocks become unbounded is
tracked, regions can be classified in four distinctclasses—Z (Zero), P (Positive),
M (Mixed), and U (Unbounded)—defined as:(i)Class Z:ℓ≥0∧r= 0∧X 0̸=∅;
(ii)Class P:ℓ≥0∧r>0∧X 0=∅;(iii)Class M:ℓ≥0∧r>0∧X 0̸=∅; and
(iv)Class U:ℓ>0∧r= 0∧X 0=∅.
ClassZdenotes regions in which some clocks may be unbounded, while all
bounded clocks have no fractional part and at least one clock must be bounded.
ClassPdenotes regions where some clocks may be unbounded, and all bounded
clocks have a fractional part greater than zero (again, at least one clock must be
bounded). ClassMdenotes regions where some clocks may be unbounded and
the bounded clocks are partitioned into those with no fractional part and those
with a fractional part greater than zero (at least two clocks must be bounded,
one with no fractional part and one with a fractional part greater than zero).
Finally, classUdenotes regions in which all clocks are unbounded. Notice that
the region presented in Example 1 belongs to classP. This classification into
different classes lies at the core of the algorithms that compute the immediate
delay successor and immediate delay predecessors of a given region.

5 Implementation and Experimental Evaluation
This section introducesTarzanalong with a detailed empirical evaluation of its
forward and backward reachability algorithms. To express reachability queries,
we use the standard Computation Tree Logic formula∃♢(φ), meaning that there
exists a path in the state space in whichφeventually holds true.
5.1 TheTarzanlibrary
Tarzanis a C++20 library for performing forward and backward reachability
analysis of TA and networks of TA, using either breadth-first (BFS) or depth-
first (DFS) state space exploration.Tarzansupports shared integer variables
(specified by using general arithmetic expressions), urgent locations, and invari-
ants. The semantics of both invariants and networks inTarzanfollows those of
Uppaal [7]. Support for networks of TA and integer variables in backward reach-
ability is currently under development. Successors and predecessors algorithms
were revised to allow each clock to have its own maximum constant.
Tarzanaccepts .txt input files, parsed using the Boost Spirit X3 library, con-
taining a TA representation written in a domain-specific language calledLiana.
Each file represents a single TA; a network of TA is defined using multiple files.
Networks of TAAnetworkof sizenis the parallel composition ofnindivid-
ual TA [4, Chapter 9]. GivennregionsR 1, . . . ,R n, each corresponding to a TA
A1, . . . ,A nin the network, anetwork region Ris defined as R:={R 1, . . . ,R n}.
To compute both immediate delay and discrete successors of R,Tarzanrelies
on specialized data structures that generalize the applicability of the algorithms
originally developed for individual regions to network regions. These data struc-
tures ensure the correctness of on-the-fly immediate delay and discrete successors
computation across the Cartesian product ofA 1, . . . ,A n. In particular, a C++
double-ended queue maintains the relative ordering of the fractional parts of all
clocks within R, partitioning these clocks into ordered equivalence classes. This
ordering captures the evolution over time of all clocks within R, including which
clocks will exit the unit (possibly becoming unbounded), reach the next unit, or
be reset. Furthermore, a C++set identifies the regions in Rthat are of classZor
M. Since the generalized algorithms for network regions operate by applying the

--- Page 11 ---

successors algorithms to a subset of individual regions in R, this classification is
essential for computing immediate delay successors; indeed, regions of classZor
Mcontain clocks that will exit the unit; hence, their immediate delay successors
must be computed prior to those of regions of classP.
Implementation considerationsSince the focus of this paper is the imple-
mentation of algorithms using the new representation of Equation (1), most op-
timizations are deferred to future work. Currently,Tarzandoes not implement
any optimizations for state space exploration, except for symmetry reduction [8].
Memory compression optimizations are not implemented either. The algorithms
also suffer when computing delay successors (predecessors), as these computa-
tions require generating a sequence of immediate delay successors (predecessors).
This limitation could be mitigated by implementing the optimization described
in Section 4 for delay predecessors (which can be adapted for delay successors as
well). Furthermore, sinceTarzanonly performs reachability analysis, proving
that a region is unreachable going forward requires exploring the entire reachable
state space. Due to the aforementioned limitations, a BFS exploration must visit
all delay successors (predecessors) before visiting discrete successors (predeces-
sors), which is typically unnecessary and increases the verification time. For this
reason, unless stated otherwise, all experiments follow a DFS strategy.

6 Conclusion and Future Works
We introducedTarzan, a region-based verification library supporting forward
and backward reachability for TA. Our experiments demonstrated thatTarzan

--- Page 16 ---
16 A. Manini, M. Rossi, P. San Pietro
exhibits superior performance on closed TA and TA with punctual guards, while
zone-based approaches remain better suited to other classes of TA. We showed
that backward reachability withTarzanis often feasible in practice and effective
for verifying safety properties. For this reason, the integration ofTarzaninto
existing tools has the potential to enhance their verification capabilities.
Future work will proceed in several directions. We plan to integrate additional
optimizations intoTarzan, with a particular focus on improving the computa-
tion of delay successors and predecessors, while leveraging symbolic techniques
to reduce memory usage. In addition, we will extend backward reachability al-
gorithms to networks of TA, thereby laying the foundation for applyingTarzan
to the synthesis of winning strategies in Timed Games. Finally, we intend to ex-
plore hybrid approaches that complementTarzanwith zone-based techniques,
thereby combining the strengths of both abstractions.
