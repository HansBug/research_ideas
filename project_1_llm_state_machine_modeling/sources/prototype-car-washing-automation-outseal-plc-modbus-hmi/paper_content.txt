ABSTRACT  
Automation technology has significantly improved the efficiency of various 
sectors, including car washing services, which traditionally require intensive 
time and labor.This research aims to design and develop a prototype of an 
automatic car washing system  using Outseal PLC and Modbus HMI to enhance 
the effectiveness and quality of car washing processes.The prototype integrates 
infrared proximity sensors, DC gearbox motors, DC pumps, DC fans, and a 
conveyor system controlled by a ladder diagram programmed t hrough Outseal 
Studio. Wireless monitoring and control are enabled using the DT -06 WiFi 
module with Modbus TCP/IP protocol. Performance testing was conducted to 
evaluate system responsiveness, sensor accuracy, and the stability of wireless 
communication be tween the PLC and HMI.The system successfully automated 
all stages of the car washing process, including soap spraying, brushing, rinsing 
with clean water, and drying, achieving response times ranging from 0.14 to 0.3 
seconds. Wireless communication remain ed stable up to a distance of 16 meters, 
and the infrared proximity sensors demonstrated high detection accuracy with an 
error margin of only 2 –3.2%.The developed prototype effectively automates the 
car washing process with accurate detection and responsiv e control, operating 
reliably under testing conditions. This system is suitable for small -scale car 
washing operations and educational laboratory applications, with potential 
scalability for broader industrial implementation with additional development in 
advanced brush mechanisms and cloud -based monitoring integration.  Article History:

INTRODUCTION  
Car washing services are essential for maintaining the cleanliness and aesthetic value of vehicles while ensuring 
their longevity. Traditionally, manual car washing processes require significant time and human resources, often 
resulting in long queues and inconsistent service quality  (Imron, 2018) . The increasing demand for efficiency and 
service quality improvement has driven the adoption of automation tech nology in the car washing industry (Oktaviani, 
Ali, & Taher, 2019) . 
Automation systems integrate mechanical, electronic, and comput er-based components to control and monitor 
processes with minimal human intervention, aiming to enhance productivity, quality, and operational efficiency. 
Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) are a critical component of industrial automation systems  (Firdaus & 
Hariyono, 2022) , enabling automatic control of devices based on predefined logic  (Irawan, 2021) . The integration of 
PLCs with Human -Machine Interfaces (HMIs) facilita tes real -time monitoring and remote control, allowing operators 
to supervise system performance and control operations efficiently from a distance  (Susanto & Kurniawan, 2021) . 
Previous studies have demonstrated the application of PLCs in various automation systems, including 
manufacturing, industrial control, and small -scale automated washing systems  (Rozaq & Rohman, 2019) . However, 
many systems primarily focus on control functions without integratin g comprehensive monitoring capabilities via HMI. 
The implementation of wireless communication using Modbus TCP/IP further enhances the flexibility of monitoring 
and controlling automated systems remotely  (Suwarno, 2020) , contributing to operational convenience and 
safety (Robiyahya, Arnandi, & Noorsetyo, 2019) . 
This research focuses on de veloping a prototype of an automatic car washing system using Outseal PLC as the 
control unit and Modbus HMI for monitoring and control purposes (Setyawan dan Endan g Sri Rahayu, Raya Bogor Km, 
& Timur, 2020) . The system is designed to automate the entire car washing process, including soap spraying, brushing, 
rinsing with clean water, and drying using a conveyor system for vehicle movement. Infrared proximity sens ors are 
utilized to detect vehicle presence and trigger the washing stages automatically, ensuring precise and timely 
operation (Suwarno , 2020) . 
The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of car washing services 
through automation while providing a practical solution for small -scale car washing operations and educational 
laboratory purposes. T he research also aims to evaluate the performance of the prototype in terms of system 
responsiveness, sensor accuracy, and wireless communication stability, providing insights into its feasibility for broader

LITERATURE REVIEW  
The utilization of Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs)  in industrial automation has been widely 
implemented, including in automatic vehicle washing systems  (Oktaviani et al., 2019)  designed a PLC and SCADA  
based automatic car washing prototype, demonstrating improved washing efficiency, although it lacked wireless 
monitoring through HMI. (Rozaq & Rohman, 2019)  explored the use of proximity sensors in automated car washing 
systems but focused only on pump motor activation without full stage control integration.  
(Firdaus & Hariyono, 2022)  highlighted the effectiveness of PLCs in controlling processes in  industrial 
applications, which is relevant for extending automation into service industries such as vehicle washing. (Suwarno, 
2020)  examined the application of Modbus HMI with Outseal PLC for wireless communication, showing stable 
monitoring performance via Bluetooth, which aligns with the development of WiFi -based Modbus TCP/IP monitoring 
in automated systems.  
(Purnomo et al., 2021)  developed an automated vertical car parking system using Outseal PLC with Android -
based Modbus HMI, demonstrating the system's monitoring and remote control capabilities ap plicable to public service 
automation. Additionally, (Setyawan dan Endang Sri Rahayu et al., 2020)  implemented utility monitoring and control 
devices using Outseal PLC and smartphones, illustrating the feasibili ty of integrating Outseal PLC for cost -effective 
remote monitoring systems.  
Existing studies indicate that PLC-based automation systems  effectively reduce manual labor dependency while 
improving service consistency in car washing operations. However, ther e remains a research gap in developing a system 
that fully integrates real-time monitoring via HMI, wireless connectivity using Modbus TCP/IP, and high -accuracy 
vehicle detection sensors within a single comprehensive prototype . This study addresses this ga p by combining 
complete control across all washing stages, high -accuracy proximity sensors, and WiFi -based HMI monitoring in a 
single system, supporting small -scale vehicle washing services and educational laboratory applications while enabling 
further dev elopment for broader industrial scalability.  
 
METHOD  
This research employed an experimental approach to design, implement, and evaluate a prototype of an 
automatic car washing system integrated with Outseal PLC and Modbus HMI for automated operation and r eal-time 
monitoring. The system was developed to enhance operational efficiency, improve washing service quality, and provide 
a reliable monitoring method for small -scale car washing services.. (Yanto, Amin, Mahmudi, Aristo, & Dewi, 2022) . 
The system integrates an infrared proximity sensor for vehicle detection, push buttons for manual contr ol, DC 
pumps for soap spraying and water rinsing, a DC gearbox motor for conveyor movement, a DC fan for the drying 
process, relays for actuator switching, a PWM module for speed regulation, and the DT -06 WiFi module for wireless 
communication (Feryando, Prasetyo, Wibowo, & Dafa Farhandicha, 2023) . he Outseal PLC functions as the main 
controller programmed using Outseal Studio software, while the  Modbus HMI allows users to monitor and control the 
system remotely through an Android device .  
For hardware integration, the system employed a 12V DC gearbox motor to drive the conveyor, two 12V DC 
pumps for soap spraying and rinsing, and a 12V DC fan fo r drying  (Utami, Zahra, & Sudjadi, 2020) . The power supply 
system converted 220V A C to 12V and 5V DC to support the operation of the components  (Saputra, Ma’arif, 
Maghfiroh, Chotikunnan, & Rahmadhia, 2023)  . The DT -06 WiFi module was configured with a static IP address to 
maintain stable wireless communicati on between the PLC and the HMI.Performance testing was conducted to evaluate 
system responsiveness, including measuring the time interval from sensor detection to actuator activation (Purnomo et 
al., 2021) . The accuracy of the infrared proximity sensors was tested at various distances to determine their detection 
precision. Additionally  (Sartono, Djamin, & Suryadi, 2024) , the stability of wireless communic ation between the PLC 
and HMI was assessed by measuring the connection delay and reliability at different distances, ensuring the system’s 
feasibility for real -time remote monitoring and control.  This method allowed for comprehensive testing of the prototype 
under practical conditions, providing valuable data on the system’s operational stability, responsiveness, and accuracy, 
which are essential parameters for validating the effectiveness of the a utomated car washing system for small -scale 
operations and potential industrial applications  (Anaam K I & Pranata R Y, Abdillah h, 2022) .

RESULT  
The prototype of the automatic car washing system using Outseal  PLC and Modbus HMI was successfully 
developed and tested according to the design specifications. The system was able to automate the entire washing 
process, including soap spraying, brushing, rinsing with clean water, and drying, while ensuring responsive  operation 
and stable monitoring using the HMI interface.  The stability of the wireless communication between the PLC and the 
HMI was evaluated using the DT -06 WiFi module configured with a static IP address. The results, as presented in Table 
1, indicate that the system maintained a stable connection up to a distance of 16 meters with acceptable response delays, 
enabling effective real -time monitoring and control. Beyond this distance, connection failures occurred, suggesting that 
optimal performance is ac hieved within this range.

Additionally, voltage measurements confirmed that the system components received stable voltage within the 
required tolerances, with the 220V AC input effectively converted to 12V and 5V DC outputs. All actuators, including 
the DC gearbox motor, DC pumps, and DC fan, operated consistently during testing, supporting the system's stability 
and operational reliability.  Overall, the test results confirm that  the developed prototype effectively automates the car 
washing process with responsive control, accurate detection, and stable wireless monitoring, demonstrating its 
feasibility for small -scale car washing operations and educational laboratory applications . 
The prototype was tested under real conditions, and the system was able to operate automatically in executing all 
washing stages with the vehicle moving through each stage on the conveyor. The integration of the Modbus HMI 
allowed the operator to monito r the status of each stage, observe actuator conditions, and control the process remotely 
using a mobile device.  
 
DISCUSSION  
The implementation of the automatic car washing system using Outseal  PLC and Modbus HMI has demonstrated 
significant potential in improving the efficiency and quality of car washing services. The integration of automation in 
the washing process reduced the dependency on manual labor while ensuring consistency across washin g stages.  The 
response times recorded between 0.14 to 0.3 seconds indicate that the system can perform real -time operations, which is 
crucial for synchronizing the sequence of washing, rinsing, and drying processes on the moving conveyor system. This 
align s with previous findings emphasizing the role of PLCs in improving process efficiency within industrial 
automation by providing rapid and precise control over actuators during operations.  
The high accuracy of the infrared proximity sensors, with error mar gins between 2% to 3.2%, further supports 
the effectiveness of the system in detecting the presence of the vehicle without physical contact, reducing mechanical 
wear and ensuring reliability over prolonged usage. Similar applications in automated systems h ave highlighted the 
advantage of non -contact sensors in improving the operational lifespan and precision of automation systems.

CONCLUSION  
This study successfully designed and implemented a prototype of an automatic car washing system utilizing 
Outseal PLC and Modbus HMI, aiming to enhance operational efficiency and ser vice quality in car washing processes. 
The system was able to automate the entire washing process, including soap spraying, brushing, rinsing, and drying, 
with responsive control and high detection accuracy using infrared proximity sensors. Wireless monito ring and control 
were effectively achieved through the DT -06 WiFi module with stable performance up to a distance of 16 meters.  The 
findings demonstrate that the developed system can operate reliably and efficiently, making it suitable for small -scale 
car washing operations and educational laboratory applications. This research contributes to the development of 
practical automation systems in service industries, highlighting the potential for further advancements through the 
integration of advanced brush me chanisms, water recycling systems, and cloud -based monitoring for enhanced 
operational management and scalability.
