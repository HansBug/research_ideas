Design and Construction of a Sliding Garage Door Powered by a 3-Phase Motor 
Controlled by a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) Based on an Outseal PLC, with A 
Remote Control as the Trigger.

 This study discusses the design and development of an automatic sliding garage 
door system powered by a three-phase electric motor and controlled via a 
Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) based on an Outseal Programmable Logic 
Controller (PLC). The system is engineered to enhance operational efficiency 
and convenience by utilizing a remote control as the primary trigger for opening 
and closing the door. Using a VFD enables flexible motor speed regulation, 
resulting in smoother, safer door movement. The Outseal PLC serves as the 
central controller, integrating signals from the remote control and automatically 
managing the operation of both the VFD and the three-phase motor. Testing 
results demonstrate that the system operates with fast response, stability, and 
reliability under various conditions. At a VFD frequency setting of 3 Hz, the 
motor runs at 90 rpm, achieving an open/close cycle time of 13 seconds. When 
the frequency is increased to 3.5 Hz, the motor speed rises to 105 rpm, reducing 
the cycle time to 11 seconds.

NOVELTY:  
The novelty of this research lies in the implementation 
of an affordable industrial-grade control architecture, 
utilizing an Outseal PLC and VFD to achieve precise 
speed regulation and high operational reliability in a 
domestic sliding door system, a configuration 
typically reserved for high-end industrial applications.  
 
1. INTRODUCTION 
1.1. Research Background  
With advances in automation technology, the demand for 
practical, efficient, and secure garage door systems continues to 
grow, especially in industrial, commercial, and modern 
residential buildings. Sliding garage doors have become a popular 
solution due to their space-saving design and ease of operation. 
However, conventional systems that rely on manual operation or motors without speed control often face issues such as jerky 
movement, high energy consumption, and increased risk of 
mechanical damage. 
To address these challenges, the use of a three-phase electric 
motor controlled by a Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) offers an 
ideal solution. VFDs enable flexible speed regulation, tailoring 
door movement to operational needs while improving energy 
efficiency. Integrating this system with an Outseal Programmable 
Logic Controller (PLC) provides precise control capabilities and 
programmable logic that can be customized to suit various 
operational scenarios.

1.2. Literature Review 
Research on garage door automation systems has seen significant 
advancements alongside advances in control and actuator 
technologies. Previous studies have explored the use of electric 
motors as the primary drivers for automatic doors, focusing on 
energy efficiency and system reliability. Three-phase motors are 
widely favored in industrial applications due to their ability to 
deliver high torque and withstand heavy workloads [1]. 
The use of Variable Frequency Drives (VFDs) in motor 
control systems has proven effective in enhancing operational 
efficiency by adjusting motor speed and torque as needed. 
Research indicates that VFDs enable smoother motion transitions 
and significantly reduce energy consumption compared to 
conventional control systems [2]. Additionally, VFDs offer 
protection against overcurrent and overheating, which are crucial 
for high-intensity automatic door systems. 
Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) are vital 
components in automation systems due to their programming 
flexibility and ability to integrate various input/output devices. 
Although relatively new to the market, the Outseal PLC has 
demonstrated competitive performance in small to medium-scale 
industrial control applications. Studies show that Outseal PLCs 
can be integrated with VFDs and external sensors to form 
responsive and adaptive control systems [3]. 
The use of a remote control as a system trigger has become a 
standard in modern automatic door design. This technology 
allows users to operate the system remotely, enhancing 
convenience and security. RF-based remote systems offer stable 
performance and sufficient range for residential and light 
industrial applications [4]. 
Overall, the existing literature supports the design of a sliding 
garage door system using a three-phase motor, VFD, Outseal 
PLC, and remote control as an efficient, user-friendly automation 
solution. This research aims to integrate these components into a 
single cohesive system and evaluate its performance under real-
world operating conditions.

1.3. Research Objective 
The objective of this study is to design and develop an automatic 
sliding garage door system that uses a three-phase electric motor 
as the main actuator, controlled by a Variable Frequency Drive 
(VFD) based on an Outseal PLC, and operated via remote control. 
Specifically, this research aims to: 
a. Design the mechanical and electronic systems of the sliding 
garage door to be compatible with a three-phase motor and 
VFD. 
b. Develop a control program based on the Outseal PLC to 
manage motor and VFD operations efficiently and safely. 
c. Integrate a remote control system as the primary trigger for 
automatic door opening and closing. 
d. Test the overall system performance, including response 
speed, operational stability, energy efficiency, and reliability 
under real working conditions. 
e. Evaluate the effectiveness of the automation system in 
enhancing the comfort, safety, and efficiency of sliding 
garage door usage.

2.2. Methods 
1. System Design 
Mechanical and electronic designs were created using CAD 
software and logic control diagrams. Wiring schematics and 
PLC programming were arranged according to the desired 
operational sequence. 
2. Component Integration 
All components were assembled based on the design. The 
three-phase motor was connected to the VFD, which the 
Outseal PLC then controlled. The remote control was 
configured to send signals to the PLC input. 
3. PLC Programming 
Control logic was programmed using Ladder Diagrams in 
Outseal Studio V3.6. The program included start/stop 
conditions, speed settings, and responses to remote signals 
and limit sensors. 
4. System Testing 
The system was tested under various scenarios: door opening 
and closing, response to remote control, motor speed, and 
protection against disturbances. Parameters such as response 
time, movement stability, and power consumption were 
recorded. 
5. Evaluation and Analysis 
Test results were analyzed to assess system effectiveness. 
Performance was compared against safety standards and 
operational comfort benchmarks for automatic doors. 
3. RESULT AND DISCUSSION 
This research successfully developed an automatic sliding garage 
door system integrated with a three-phase electric motor, VFD, 
Outseal PLC, and a remote control as the trigger. The testing 
process evaluated the system’s performance in terms of 
functionality, efficiency, and reliability. 
System Testing Results: 
3.1. Remote Control Response 
The system responded to signals from the remote control with an 
average delay of less than 1 second. The door’s open and close 
functions operated as commanded, indicating stable 
communication between the remote and the PLC.

The three-phase motor controlled by the VFD demonstrated 
smooth and stable operation. Motor speed could be adjusted via 
VFD parameters, allowing the door to move at a safe speed 
without sudden jolts. 
 At a VFD frequency of 3 Hz and motor speed of 90 rpm, 
the door took 13 seconds to open or close. 
 At 3.5 Hz and 105 rpm, the time reduced to 11 seconds. 
 Frequencies above 4 Hz affected speed and door inertia, 
causing the door to bounce upon reaching its final 
position or stop point. 
3.3. Outseal PLC Programming 
The control logic programmed into the Outseal PLC operated 
according to the designed scenario. The PLC successfully 
managed motor sequences, read inputs from the remote and 
sensors, and delivered accurate outputs to the VFD. 
3.4. Limit Sensors and Protection 
Limit sensors functioned effectively to stop door movement at the 
end positions. The VFD’s protection system also activated during 
current surges, safeguarding the motor from damage. 
3.5. Energy Efficiency 
The use of VFDs reduced power consumption by 20–30% 
compared to direct-motor systems without speed control. This 
demonstrates significant energy savings 
 
The integration of a three-phase motor, a Variable Frequency 
Drive (VFD), and an Outseal PLC provides flexibility and precise 
control for the sliding garage door system. Using a remote control 
as the trigger enhances operational convenience for users, 
especially in situations with high mobility or unfavourable 
weather conditions. 
The success of this system demonstrates that automation 
technology based on PLC and VFD can be effectively applied in 
both residential and light industrial settings. Furthermore, the 
system has potential for future development, including additional 
features such as safety sensors, IoT connectivity, and integration 
with smart home platforms. 
Based on the testing results, the system exhibits stable, 
responsive, and energy-efficient performance. This confirms that 
the design and implementation have met the research objectives 
and are suitable for practical application. 
In this study, only  two frequency settings were used on the 
VFD: 
 At 3 Hz, the motor speed was 90 rpm, requiring 13 seconds 
to open or close the door. 
 At 3.5 Hz, the motor speed increased to 105 rpm, reducing 
the time to 11 seconds. 
In the event of a power outage from the national grid (PLN), 
when no current reaches the control box, the door can still be 
easily opened manually by simply pushing it. This is possible 
because the system does not use a gearbox, making the door 
lightweight and easy to operate. 4. CONCLUSION 
This research successfully designed and developed an automatic 
sliding garage door system using a three-phase electric motor as 
the main actuator, controlled by a VFD based on an Outseal PLC, 
and operated via remote control. Testing results indicate that the 
system performs effectively and responsively, with smooth door 
movement, efficient energy consumption, and precise control. At 
a VFD frequency of 3.5 Hz, the induction motor reached 105 rpm, 
completing the opening or closing cycle in 11 seconds. The 
integration of a VFD and an Outseal PLC allows motor speed 
adjustment to meet operational needs, provides protection against 
electrical disturbances, and, by using a remote control as the 
primary trigger, provides users with convenient access to operate 
the door from a distance. Overall, the design and implementation 
have fulfilled the research objectives and demonstrate strong 
potential for application in garage door automation systems for 
industrial buildings and modern homes. This study may serve as 
a foundation for further development toward smarter systems 
integrated with Internet of Things (IoT) technologies or smart 
home platforms
