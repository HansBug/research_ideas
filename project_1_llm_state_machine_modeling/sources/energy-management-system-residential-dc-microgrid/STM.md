### 2. 基于原文整理后的自然语言描述

The residential DC microgrid is supervised by a ten-state `Global Management Controller` rather than by a loose priority list. Its transitions are driven jointly by bus-voltage bands, PV power thresholds, main-grid availability, and battery charge or discharge status, while each state simultaneously defines the output modes of the PV interface, the battery `DC-DC` converter, the grid-side `DC-AC` converter, and the local-load connection. From `START`, the controller branches into different operating states depending on whether PV alone can sustain the bus, whether the main grid is available, and whether the battery is charging or can discharge. The transition tables then refine the logic with states `1-9`, including low-voltage recovery states that turn the grid-side converter into `RECTIFIER`, battery-support states that set the `DC-DC` stage to `BOOST`, inverter-export states, and cut-off states such as `STATE 7` where converters are turned off and loads are taken out. The stated priority order is also explicit: keep the DC bus in range, extract maximum PV power, use the main grid for deficits before the battery only when appropriate, and redirect surplus first to the battery and then to the grid. Because the paper gives both the input guards and the converter-mode outputs for every state, the GMC is a detailed EFSM for residential power routing rather than a generic EMS narrative.
