>
> The requirements are satisﬁed with PED CLEAR, TFL GREEN and INT OK, respectively.

#### 摘录 D

- 出处：第 5 页，`A. State Machine`
> Because of the parallel architecture of our state machine, we implemented a handshaking protocol where the a request is sent to the target state, and the state is changed only when a positive response is received.
>
> BUSY sub-state means a request for connection has been sent for that state and a response is expected.

#### 摘录 E

- 出处：第 6 页，`C. Behavior Planning`
> If the stopping requirements include PEDESTRIAN, then the stop server has to wait until PED CLEAR event is received.
>
> If the requirements include INT, ego car has to consider other vehicles and safely make the turn. We use a heuristic policy that uses a time to collision (TTC) threshold to decide when to cross.

> The vehicle detects a pedestrian near the roadway and issues a PEDESTRIAN event, resulting transition to the STOP state ... the vehicle senses that the pedestrian has cleared the roadway (PED CLEAR) before transitioning back to the GO state.

### 2. 基于原文整理后的自然语言描述

The GoMentum urban automated-driving system uses a hierarchical state machine as its top-level event supervisor, with five main states: `NOT READY`, `ROUTE PLAN`, `GO`, `STOP`, and `ERROR`. After the user enters a destination, the controller moves from `NOT READY` to `ROUTE PLAN`; once a route is successfully found, it enters `GO` and commands the vehicle to continue in the current lane at an appropriate speed. The supervisor continuously evaluates incoming events, and any `PEDESTRIAN`, `TFL RED`, or `INT` event raises the `MUST STOP` flag and forces a transition into `STOP`, where the active stop requirements are stored until their paired clearance events arrive. These requirements are discharged by `PED CLEAR`, `TFL GREEN`, and `INT OK`, with the intersection branch using a time-to-collision threshold over cross traffic to decide when it is safe to leave the stop state. The same supervisor is demonstrated on real urban scenarios: the vehicle stops at a no-turn-on-red intersection until the light turns green, waits at unsignalized right turns until cross traffic creates an acceptable gap, detours around construction pylons when an obstacle-free path exists, and remains stopped for both visible and initially occluded pedestrians until the road boundary is clear again.
