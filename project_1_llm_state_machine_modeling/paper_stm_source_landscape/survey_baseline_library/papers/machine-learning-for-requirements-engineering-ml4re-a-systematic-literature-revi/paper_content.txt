Systematic literature reviewA B S T R A C T
Context: The research of machine learning for requirements engineering (ML4RE) has attracted more and
more attention from researchers and practitioners. Although pioneering research has shown the potential of
using ML techniques to improve RE practices, there lacks a systematic and comprehensive literature review in
academia that integrates an industrial perspective. Specifically, none of the reviews available in ML4RE have
considered the grey literature, which is primarily from practitioner origin and is more reflective of the real
issues and challenges faced in practice.
Objective: In this paper, we conduct a systematic survey of academic publications in ML4RE and complement
it with the practitioners’ voices from Stack Overflow to complete a comprehensive literature review. Our
research objective is to provide a comprehensive view of the current research progress in ML4RE, present the
main questions and challenges faced in RE practice, understand the gap between research and practice, and
provide our insights into how the RE academic domain can pragmatically develop in the future.
Method: We systematically investigated 207 academic papers on ML4RE from 2010 to 2022, along with 375
questions related to RE practices on Stack Overflow and their corresponding answers. Our analysis encompassed
their trends, focused RE activities and tasks, employed solutions, and associated data. Finally, we conducted
a joint analysis, contrasting the outcomes of both parts.
Results: Based on the statistical results from collected literature, we summarize an academic roadmap and
analyse the disparities, offering research recommendations. Our suggestions include the development of
intelligent question-answering assistants employing large language models, the integration of machine learning
into industrial tools, and the promotion of collaboration between academia and industry.
Conclusion: This study contributes by providing a holistic view of ML4RE, delineating disparities between
research and practice, and proposing pragmatic suggestions to bridge the academia-industry gap.
1. Introduction
Requirements engineering (RE) is an imperative step in the early
stage of software engineering (SE). Although researchers have been
continuously investigating methods and techniques to facilitate require-
ments practices, the entire process of requirements engineering still
requires a large amount of manual effort (e.g., eliciting stakeholder
requirements via interview or classifying requirements according to
certain requirements taxonomy). A primary reason for this is that RE
activities typically require in-depth domain knowledge and advanced
analysis skills, which is difficult to be fully automated.
In recent years, the rapid development of machine learning (ML)
technology has been propelled by improvements in computational
power. The successful applications of ML in areas such as natural

Previous research has explored the ML4RE. Iqbal et al. [2] con-
ducted a survey to obtain an overview of how ML techniques can
help with RE activities. Additionally, Zamani et al. [3] performed a
mapping study of ML applications in RE, analysing 65 papers to assess
ML’s effectiveness in automating RE tasks. Their works focused on the
entire RE process, shedding light on how ML techniques influence and
streamline various phases. Besides, some research delves into specific
RE activities or tasks. For instance, Lim et al. [4] reviewed current
state-of-the-art approaches to data-driven requirements elicitation from
dynamic data sources.
We notice that these studies have predominantly focused on aca-
demic publications and missed integrating insights from grey literature
sources, such as industry blogs and forums. In contrast to academic
publications that are mainly published by researchers, grey literature
is constantly produced by practitioners on a great scale, shedding light
on the ‘‘state of the practice’’ [5]. As indicated in [6], integrating
grey literature into systematic literature reviews can bridge the gap
between academic research and industry practices, offering a more
holistic view of challenges and solutions. Although there is an increas-
ing number of systematic literature reviews considering grey literature
in SE studies [7,8], they are not as prevalent in RE.
To fill the gap in the RE field, this paper aims to conduct a sys-
tematic literature review on ML4RE, integrated with insights from
grey literature sourced from Stack Overflow. Specifically, our literature
review consists of three parts. The white part involved a review of
207 papers published between 2010 and 2022, specifically focusing
on ML4RE. Meanwhile, the grey part encompassed an analysis of
375 questions and their corresponding answers gathered from Stack
Overflow discussions on RE practices during the same period. Lastly,
ourjoint analysis involved contrasting the outcomes of these two
parts to discern their similarities and differences. We analyse the lit-
erature’s trends, RE activities, RE tasks, solutions, and data. Research
results indicate that both exhibit substantial interest in RE analysis and
requirements documentation. Beyond commonalities, the white part
tends to focus on RE elicitation, while the grey part places greater
emphasis on RE management. The white part underscores the use of
ML technologies such as SVM, CNN, and BERT. In contrast, the grey
part relies more on tools such as Microsoft TFS, Jira, and IBM Rational
DOORS, with ML techniques such as LDA, POS, TF–IDF, and neural
networks. Additionally, the grey part pays particular attention to the
user story and use case, areas relatively less explored in the white
part. Based on these findings, we summarize an academic roadmap
and conduct a detailed analysis of the disparities between the white
part and the grey part. Subsequently, we propose research suggestions,
advocating for the development of intelligent question-answering as-
sistants using large language models and the integration of machine
learning into industrial tools. We further encourage enhanced collabo-
ration between academia and industry to gain a deeper understanding
of real-world research problems and data.
In summary, this paper makes the following contributions. Firstly,
we provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of ML4RE
research. Secondly, we investigate the real-world circumstances of RE
practitioners through grey literature obtained from Stack Overflow.
Thirdly, we highlight the gap between research and practice in the
ML4RE domain, particularly in areas less attended to by researchers.
Lastly, to bridge the gap between industry and academia, we propose
practical research suggestions in ML4RE.
In the remaining part of this paper, we present the related work in

Finally, we conclude the paper in Section 9.2. Related work
The related work of our literature review in ML4RE comes from
two sources. The first set of reviews pertains to ML4RE. The second
set consists of systematic literature reviews in the field of SE. In the
following two subsections, we will present the related work in detail
from these two sources.
2.1. Literature reviews about ML4RE
In this section, we present a compilation of relevant literature
reviews focusing on ML4RE. We have found several literature reviews,
some addressing the entirety of the RE process, while others concen-
trate on specific RE activities or tasks. Therefore, this subsection is
divided into two parts to introduce these works in detail.
2.1.1. Entire requirements engineering process
Two papers focus on the entire requirements engineering process
and provide an overview of how machine learning techniques play a
role in the various stages of requirements engineering. Iqbal et al. [2]
surveyed many research papers to obtain an overview of how ML
is helping RE. They observed the impact of ML in all five phases
of RE and discussed the specific problems solved by ML, the fea-
tures, ML algorithms, and datasets. However, the papers they surveyed
were not obtained from a systematic search process, and therefore,
the conclusions drawn may not be systematic and comprehensive.
Zamani et al. [3] performed a mapping study of ML applications in
RE, analysing 65 papers to assess ML’s effectiveness in automating RE
tasks. It identifies these studies’ techniques, challenges, datasets, and
evaluation metrics. Compared to our work’s comprehensive review,
this paper focuses more on the empirical aspects of ML in RE, offering
specific insights into ML’s practical efficacy in this field. Besides, Zhao
et al. [9] focused on NLP4RE, analysing 404 studies to understand the
application of NLP in RE. Because there are overlaps between NLP and
ML, we regard this research as one of the related works to analyse.
2.1.2. Part of requirements engineering process
In the requirement elicitation process, G.C. Sampada et al. [10]
provided an overview of different approaches to automate require-
ment elicitation and specification in the software development cy-
cle. Lim et al. [4] reviewed the current state-of-the-art approaches to
data-driven requirements elicitation from dynamic data sources and
identified research gaps. Cheligeer et al. [11] selected 86 papers to
summarize and analyse studies incorporating ML and NLP into demand
elicitation. They categorized the techniques for constructing ML-based
requirement elicitation methods into five parts.
In the requirements classification process, Alrumaih et al. [12]
presented some research studies on requirements classification and
discussed limitations to yield suggestions for improvement. Perez et al.
[13] identified the applications of ML techniques in classifying software
requirements by 13 selected papers and summarized the most recurrent
classification algorithms and the most frequent training datasets. Khe-
lifa et al. [14] investigated the applicability of machine learning tech-
niques in software requirements classification and requirements change
requests classification. Additionally, Kadebu et al. [15] concentrated on
security requirements engineering and explored the application of ML
techniques in security requirements extraction and classification.
In the requirements management process, Xu et al. [16] summarized
eight ML methods used in requirements management and determined
18 evaluation indexes for requirements management in the ML method.
Their work serves as a foundational understanding of a wide array
of ML techniques used in managing requirements, while some stud-
ies delve deeper into specific tasks. Achimugu et al. [17] reviewed
techniques for prioritizing software requirements through 73 related
articles and concluded several limitations in existing prioritization tech-
niques. Moreover, Li et al. [18] conducted a systematic mapping study

2.2. Literature reviews in SE
In the field of SE, there is a plethora of systematic literature reviews.
We have selected the relevant papers that integrate grey literature. We
categorized them into three domains based on the paper topics, which
will be introduced one by one below.
2.2.1. Methodologies in software development
Methodologies in software development are software engineering
development models or value systems embraced by broad developers
in the software development process. Well-known methodologies in
software development include Agile, DevOps, and one brought up
based on the latter, DevSecOps. França et al. [19] performed the
literature review aiming at characterizing DevOps from multiple per-
spectives. Amaro et al. [20] aimed to clarify DevOps capabilities and
their relationship with DevOps practices. With the development of
DevOps, security is becoming increasingly important to software en-
gineering. DevSecOps comes up with the integration of modernized
security methods and DevOps. Myrbakken and Colomo-Palacios [8]
conducted the literature review to give an overview of the definition,
significance, benefits, and challenges of DevSecOps. For DevSecOps
implementation quality, Prates et al. [21] performed the literature
review to identify metrics teams can use to measure the effectiveness
of DevSecOps methodology implementation inside organizations. More
and more IT companies are adopting microservices architecture to
deliver their business. Soldani et al. [22] systematically selected and
analysed the industrial grey literature on the pains and gains of mi-
croservices. Bhandari and Colomo-Palacios [23] focused on holacracy
for software development teams. Some works focus on how to use ML to
assist DevOps in development. Recupito [24] conducted a multi-vocal
literature review to explore MLOps tools and their functionalities in
automating Machine Learning pipelines with DevOps practices.
2.2.2. General software engineering
Some research works have focused on adopting voices from practi-
tioners in a more general software engineering field. Kamei et al. [25]
conducted a tertiary study to understand how secondary studies use
grey literature. Considering the state of researchers in software en-
gineering were still struggling with research relevance and utility to
practitioners, Garousi et al. [26] carried out the literature review. They
found insights into the causes of low relevance and suggestions for
improving it. Rainer and Williams [27] conducted a tertiary study on
the studies empirically investigating software practice using blog-like
documents. Alves et al. [28] presented a comprehensive taxonomy of
practices used in the industry for building machine learning systems,
offering valuable insights to organizations for improving and managing
their ML processes and practices. Heiland et al. [29] provided an
overview of design patterns for AI-based systems, including both new
and adapted patterns, collected through a multi-vocal literature review.
2.2.3. Specific parts in software engineering
Literature reviews exist in various parts of SE. In the domain of
software testing, Raulamo-Jurvanen et al. [30] carried out a grey
literature review to identify the potential pitfalls of existing processes
and opportunities for comprehensive tool evaluations. Still, for test
automation, Garousi and Mäntylä [7] performed the literature review
on when and what to automate in software testing. Garousi et al. [31]
focused on test maturity assessment and test process improvement andconducted the literature review. Garousi and Küçük [32] performed a
multi-vocal literature mapping on test smells in both the scientific and
grey literature. Felderer and Garousi [33] surveyed software testing
in both industry and academia and they proposed their reflections
on improving the link and synergies between industry and academia
in software testing. Regarding the RE domain, Tripathi et al. [34]
used the literature review to find scientific and grey literature. They
explored how software startups used RE elicitation, documentation,
prioritization, and validation.
Overall, the landscape of SE has seen substantial developments
in the systematic literature reviews, with numerous excellent works
combining perspectives from grey literature for analysis. However, our
analysis reveals that among these works, they have yet to delve into
the ML4RE, while our work fills this gap.
3. Research protocol
We applied the standard protocol structure of systematic mapping
study in software engineering developed by Kitchenham et al. [35].
Our study consisted of a white part and a grey part , each reviewed
separately. We then developed a joint part to combine findings from
both parts.
3.1. Objectives and research questions
In particular, we have used the Goal-Question-Metric methodol-
ogy [36]. Our objectives encompass a thorough examination of the
ML4RE research landscape. Additionally, we seek to investigate practi-
cal needs in RE and methods in ML drawn from grey literature. Finally,
we compare academic research’s focus on RE in the problem domain
and the methods employed in the solution domain with real-world
practices in Stack Overflow. These objectives lead to the formulation
of research questions (RQs).
RQ1: What is the current state of academic research in ML4RE?
RQ2: What is the current state of ML4RE practices in Stack Over-
flow?
RQ3: How do Stack Overflow insights on ML4RE align with or differ
from academic research findings?
In order to answer these RQs, we define a series of tags as metrics to
specify the data or information that needs to be extracted from papers.
In the subsequent sections, we detail the literature search processes for
the white and grey parts. We also explain the metrics used to answer
the RQs and the data extraction and synthesis process.

3.2.1. Systematic search
We conduct our white part systematic search using the pre-defined
search string in four selected academic databases. The systematic
search process in the white part includes three steps. We explain each
step in the order shown in Fig. 1.

influence the quality of the systematic literature review. As suggested
by many studies [35,37], search sources should include multiple au-
thoritative databases. Specifically, we select four academic databases:
IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, and Scopus. These
four databases can cover the majority of the literature related to
requirements engineering [38].
Search string definition. The essential theme of our study is
machine learning for requirements engineering. We divide the search
string into two parts: the RE part and the ML technique part. These two
parts are connected using the ‘‘AND’’ operator. Specifically, each part

removing all of the duplications, there are 1845 papers left.
3.2.2. Systematic filter
The initial search results from those academic search engines con-
tain lots of unwanted cases, such as keynotes, posters, or papers that
are too short. In addition, different search engines perform our search
string on different scales, although we have tried to limit the search
string to the Title, Abstract, and Keywords fields. Thus, we define
three pre-exclusion criteria (PEC) to eliminate such papers to relieve
the subsequent workload. Due to the utilization of Google Sheets
for recording papers, these straightforward exclusion criteria can beautomatically applied through functions. It is worth noting that for
PEC3, we employed regex matching for the title, abstract, and keywords
of each paper against the predefined search string. After systematic
filtering, 1107 papers were left.
–PEC1 The length of the paper is less than six pages.
–PEC2 The paper is not written in English.
–PEC3 The combined content of the Title, Abstract, and Keywords in
the paper does not match our search string.
3.2.3. Three-round selection
All the papers that pass the automated filtering are then manually
evaluated for relevance. The overall selection criterion we defined is
that the paper should propose an approach that primarily uses machine
learning to assist RE-related tasks.
Inclusion and exclusion criteria. To better enforce the selection
criterion, we have decomposed it into a series of inclusion criteria (IC)
and exclusion criteria (EC) shown in Table 2. If the paper meets any
exclusion criteria, we exclude it; and only if it meets all the inclusion
criteria, we will include it.
Selection protocol. Two authors reviewed each paper regarding
the inclusion and exclusion criteria. We first conducted a pilot study on
40 papers picked from the dataset randomly. If there were conflicting
opinions, the two authors would explicitly express their concerns and
resolve them. We used this pilot study to improve our criteria and
enable the two authors to agree on the practical details. After reaching
a consensus on the selection of the papers, the two authors selected the
remaining papers. If there were any conflicts that the two authors could
not resolve, we would bring in a third researcher for additional review.
Selection process. As shown in Fig. 1, we divide the manual
inspection process into three rounds. In Round 1 , reviewers read only
the title and abstract of each paper to decide whether to exclude it. We
move all the remaining papers to the next round. In Round 2 , reviewers
read both the introduction and conclusion of all the remaining papers.
Same as before, we retain these papers until the next round, except
for those excluded papers. In Round 3 , reviewers need to read the full
text and then make the final decisions. After the three-round manual
selection, we selected 203 papers.

3.2.5. Snowball search
To make our study more comprehensive, we conducted a snow-

3.3.2. Tag-based filtering
We found a total of 23,758,292 questions on the Stack Overflow.

filter questions related to requirements engineering. Specifically, we
selected six tags, they are: requirements ,user-stories ,system-requirements ,

Duplication Removal. In Stack Overflow, questions can be associ-

selected tags. After the duplication, there are 861 questions left.
3.3.3. Manual selection
Our focus is on practical challenges faced by RE and deriving
practical requirements from them. Therefore, all 861 questions that
successfully passed tag-based filtering are then manually evaluated for
their relevance to RE. Two authors evaluated each question in terms
of the selection criterion. An initial pilot study involving 50 randomly
chosen questions was conducted to address conflicting opinions, allow-
ing the authors to reach a consensus. Subsequently, the two authors
evaluated the remaining questions. Eventually, 375 questions were
selected for the grey part.2

3.4.1. White data extraction and synthesis
To address RQ1, we intend to extract four aspects of information
from the papers, each encompassing a set of tags as illustrated in Fig. 3.
The descriptions of these four aspects and their corresponding tags are
outlined below.
Basic information. This aspect is designed to understand the basic
situation of the papers, including four tags: Publication years ,Publication
types,Publication venues , and Citations .
Problem domain. This aspect aims to understand the academic
community’s focus on requirements engineering, including two tags:
RE activities and RE tasks . For RE activities , according to the software
engineering body of knowledge (SWEBOK), we classify each paper
into one of five activities: requirements elicitation ,requirements analysis ,
requirements specification ,requirements validation and requirements man-
agement . For RE tasks , we extract the specific RE task from each paper
according to its description.
Solution domain. This aspect is to investigate the usage of machine
learning methods in ML4RE, including three tags: ML tasks ,ML tech-
niques , and ML tools . For ML tasks , we extract ML task types from each
paper, e.g., classification, and clustering. Then, we extract particular
ML techniques and ML tools used in each paper. For Reproducibility , we
record the reproduction material types provided in each paper: data,
model codes , and experiment artifacts .
Data. This aspect is to study the data analysed in ML4RE, including
two tags: Data types and Data sources . For Data types , we initially
categorize the data into one of four general types and then record the
specific data based on the content of the paper. The four general types
of data are: requirement artifacts ,user voices ,domain data , and artifacts
in SE . For Data sources , we classify into one of five categories: public ,
crawled ,private ,original , and no mention . In addition, we extract the
specific data sources.

is noticeably prominent in the grey, exhibiting a stable trend over the
years, whereas the white sector did not demonstrate substantial interest
in this activity. Moreover, specification shows a declining trend in the
grey, which did not get attention in the white part.

6.5. The similarities and differences between academic research and Stack

In summary, there is an increasing trend of academic papers while
a decline in posted questions of the Stack Overflow community. Both

suggestions.

7.1. Riding the wave of technological advancements
When analysing the selected papers, we notice significant trends
and shifts closely linked to the advancement of technology. Firstly, we
observed a significant increase in the number of ML4RE-related papers.
This trend aligns with the rapid development of machine learning
techniques and also correlates with the growth trend of the number
of applications over the past 13 years [ 40,41]. As the number of
applications increases, the demand for standing out in the competitive
market becomes critical, making requirements even more crucial. Si-
multaneously, this surge has resulted in a large volume of analyzable
data, with ML technology serving as an effective analytical method,
driving the research in ML4RE.
The selected papers also follow a clear trajectory across different
stages with the evolution of machine learning technology. We have
summarized the roadmap as depicted in Fig. 19. In the early phase,
spanning from 2010 to 2015, the focus primarily centred on traditional
machine learning algorithms such as SVM and Bayesian methods. Sub-
sequently, around 2016 to 2017, the research emphasis shifted towards
foundational neural networks, like CNN, alongside word embedding
techniques like word2vec. Then, from 2017 to 2019, there was a surge
in the application of deep learning methods, notably the adoption of
LSTM. Most recently, spanning from 2020 to 2022, attention gradually
pivoted towards generative and pre-trained models such as BERT and
GPT. It is anticipated that future research in ML4RE will continue to
explore large language models extensively.
Moreover, although public data remains the largest source of data,
the growth in research using such data has shown a slowdown in the
past two years. Conversely, there has been a significant uptick in studies
utilizing crawled data. One possible reason for this may be attributed to
the era of big data and technological advancement. The omnipresence
of digital technology in daily life generates copious amounts of data,
facilitating researchers’ study for more comprehensive and realistic
data sources.
These observations in the ML4RE research response to technological
advancements and provide valuable insights and directions for future
studies.

7.2. Contrasting priorities between academic research and practices in Stack
Overflow
In our comparative analysis of the academic research and prac-
tices in Stack Overflow in addressing issues in the RE domain, a
distinct disparity emerges. Based on the findings, researchers tend to
prefer advanced machine learning and deep learning (DL) techniques.
Conversely, practitioners are observed to lean towards the utiliza-
tion of existing software tools and natural language processing (NLP)
techniques.
The underlying reason behind this disparity may lie in the aca-
demic emphasis on the innovation and complexity of technologies,
whereas industry places greater importance on practicality and cost-
effectiveness. ML and DL techniques utilized in the white part often
entail high complexity and cost, requiring specialized knowledge and
professionals for development and implementation. Additionally, these
techniques typically demand substantial training data and computa-
tional resources. In contrast, the software tools and NLP techniques
used in the grey part are generally more straightforward to implement
and apply, as they have already been widely adopted in the industry
and possess lower learning curves and costs.
Furthermore, only a portion of the questions and answers in the
grey part mention ML techniques. This could be due to some questions
revolving around defining the concepts, methods, and tools of RE.
Therefore, focusing solely on RE domain knowledge is sufficient to

7.3. Disconnection between academic research and industrial practice
When comparing the focus on RE activities and data between the
white and grey parts, we identified disparities that underscore a dis-
connection between academia and industry.
From Fig. 17, a disparity in attention within RE activities becomes
evident. The grey part consistently raises some questions related to RE
management, while the academic research in the white part does not
show a marked increase in this domain. This difference might stem from
inadequate communication between academia and industry, resulting
in a lack of understanding within academia regarding the specific needs
and directions in RE management.
Moreover, in Fig. 18, the grey part shows significant attention
toward the user story and use case, aspects that receive relatively
less research focus within academia. This lack of emphasis on these
aspects in academic research also implies a potential disconnection
between academia and industry. Furthermore, it might be associated
with the sensitivity of these data regarding company privacy, making
it challenging to acquire sufficient support and data for academic
research.
Overall, while academia strives to explore novel methodologies,
these approaches might not always immediately resonate with the
practical problems and direct requirements of the industry. These dis-
parities highlight the necessity for a closer alignment between research
directions and real-world needs.
7.4. Suggestions
In this subsection, we plan to provide several suggestions based on
our findings and discussions for researchers in the ML4RE field.
7.4.1. Constructing intelligent question-answering assistants
Following research on the grey part, it was found that questions
about specific problems in practicing RE and conceptual definition
account for 36.2% and 8.2%, respectively. This indicates that there
are some questions that could be resolved by leveraging specialized
knowledge in the field of RE. Given the current advancements in
technology, developing intelligent question-answering assistants based
on Large Language Models (LLM) is a promising research direction.
LLM represents a class of powerful NLP tools harnessing extensive
pre-training and deep learning techniques. These models possess the
capability to deeply understand, reason, and generate natural lan-
guage text. This capability enables LLM to extract knowledge from
vast textual data, understand context, and consequently engage in
logical reasoning to generate accurate responses. Thus, constructing
an intelligent question-answering assistant using LLM offers several
distinct advantages: (1) a comprehensive repository of domain-specific
knowledge stemming from LLM’s extensive pre-training; (2) superior
comprehension and reasoning abilities, allowing for precise resolution
of complex RE problems; (3) real-time and detailed responsiveness in
providing efficient and meticulous answers to questions.
Given these advantages, it is recommended to organize datasets
from the RE domain and then fine-tune LLM toward developing an
intelligent question-answering assistant. Such an assistant could effec-
tively discern and resolve intricate concepts, offering tailored solutions
for specific RE questions.
7.4.2. Bridging machine learning with industry tools
In light of our earlier discussions, it is apparent that the practitioners
in the Stack Overflow community is inclined toward utilizing estab-
lished tools such as Microsoft TFS, Jira, IBM Rational DOORS, Rally,and Azure DevOps. However, as indicated by the statistical findings in
Section 5.3.1, these tools are frequently referenced within the context
of questions, presenting a 25% probability. This signifies the potential
challenges and complexities practitioners face while using them. For
instance, Question ID: 71746782, Title: ‘‘How to pass value of a field
from Parent Work Item to Child in Azure DevOps?’’, illustrates the
issues that may arise when utilizing these tools.
Hence, researchers are encouraged to develop intelligent adaptive
tools that integrate machine learning methods. Leveraging the foun-
dation of existing industry tools, these adaptive tools aim to address
the challenges encountered in the industrial execution of requirements
engineering tasks.
Furthermore, to advance the broader adoption of machine learn-
ing methods in industrial settings, researchers should actively support
open-source initiatives. This involves contributing machine learning
models, algorithms, and tools to a wider community. Our research
uncovered that approximately 37 out of 207 selected papers pro-
vided model codes or experimental artifacts. Given the complexity
of industrial practitioners reproducing code from academic papers, it
is recommended that researchers translate innovative methods into
useable tools, facilitating easier adoption within industrial settings.
7.4.3. Enhancing collaboration between academic research and industrial
practice
Based on the discernible disparities in focus between academic
research and the practices within Stack Overflow, we propose fortifying
collaboration between these spheres to better align research endeavours
with practical demands.
Our research reveals the heightened attention to RE management
and user story by practitioners within Stack Overflow, areas that remain
relatively unexplored within academic investigations. This discrep-
ancy may stem from a disconnection between academia and industry,
leading to a lack of understanding regarding the pressing needs of
the industrial landscape. Thus, fostering close collaboration becomes
pivotal. Conducting targeted research or surveys within industrial set-
tings would enable researchers to delve deeper into genuine industry
requisites, facilitating the identification of research directions. Simul-
taneously, collaborating with practitioners can furnish datasets from
real-world scenarios, bridging the gaps in academic access to user story
and ensuring research aligns with practical requirements.
Furthermore, the provision of more open datasets is crucial in the
research community. It is noteworthy that while there are some papers
that explicitly state the datasets they used, the accessibility of these
datasets can become an issue over time. For example, the address of
tera-PROMISE4in the paper P096 we selected is currently inaccessible.
A recommended practice is to share datasets on platforms like Zenodo
and GitHub. These established platforms contribute to the long-term
stability and accessibility of shared data.
8. Threats to validity
In this section, we analyse threats that might affect the validity of
our work. Here we adopt a classification scheme from Runeson and
Höst [42] that focuses on case study research in software engineering.
8.1. Construct validity
Construct validity reflects the extent to which the research protocol
meets the research objectives and research questions. The representa-
tiveness of selected studies is the key threat to our study. For the grey
literature review, we chose Stack Overflow as the information source.
However, when searching using a combination of RE and ML keywords,
the results were quite limited. We found that the questions retrieved

and thus, the corresponding answers were typically tailored to those
specific scenarios. Although these answers seldom included content
related to ML, we believe that these questions still help in analysing
the challenges of real-world RE. Therefore, we decided to adopt two
approaches for the grey part search: on the one hand, searching for
questions directly related to RE, and on the other hand, combining
the ML keywords with words that have some relevance to RE. We
realize that this search method may not cover all the questions related
to ML4RE, but we made our best effort to balance workload and
content quality. We look forward to collecting more comprehensive
grey resources in future research to further enrich our literature review.
8.2. Internal validity
Internal validity requires researchers to consider the factors that
influence research as comprehensively as possible. If there is a factor
that affects the quality of the study and we are not aware of that, there
will be a threat to the internal validity. Our literature review design
follows the guidelines of standard academic papers, which, to some
extent, alleviates threats to internal validity. However, potential threats
to internal validity still exist, particularly concerning the accuracy of
manually labelled tags. During the process, a large amount of data
needs to be counted and extracted. These tasks can be tedious and
may result in inaccuracies or errors in recording data. To alleviate
this issue, we created a shared spreadsheet, established labelling rules,
and minimized the need for manual input, thus reducing the risk of
errors by researchers. Additionally, we double-check the data to ensure
its accuracy and utilize computational formulas whenever possible for
statistical calculations. We believe this helps mitigate threats during the
data collection process.
8.3. External validity
External validity reflects the ability to generalize the results of
the study. One potential issue with external validity is the limited
time frame of data. In our analysis, we utilized data from 2010 to
2022, including both white and grey literature, but did not consider
studies published prior to 2010. Fortunately, the rapid advancement of
machine learning since 2010 has helped reduce this concern. Another
threat can be the limited scope of grey literature. Our primary con-
tribution lies in providing an integrated perspective on grey literature.
Stack Overflow is one of the most informative and suitable data sources,
which best fits our needs in this paper. We made efforts to encompass a
wide range of RE questions to mitigate the impact of Stack Overflow as
the sole data source. However, it is essential to acknowledge that the
generalizability of our findings may be influenced by the limitations
of Stack Overflow. To address this, future work could gradually incor-
porate additional data sources to enhance the external validity of our
study.
8.4. Conclusion validity
Conclusion validity concerns the reproducibility of the study. That
means whether other researchers following the protocol of our paper
and conducting the same study can obtain the same results. One threat
could be related to bias in the data selection. To mitigate the threat,
we have specified the inclusion and exclusion criteria for the data in
both white and grey parts and have strictly enforced them. Two authors
completed the data selection process and carried out a pilot study to
align their views before starting. We introduced a third researcher to
resolve the conflicts between the two. A similar threat exists in the
subsequent data tagging process. Before we started processing the data,
we clearly defined what data we needed to extract and how to extract
it to mitigate this threat. As with the paper selection, two authors com-
pleted the process and followed the same conflicts resolution processas in the paper selection. Besides, the researchers established a gold
standard for labelling and used strict inclusion/exclusion criteria to
unify differences among different researchers. In particular, the two
researchers tagged papers simultaneously and periodically unified their
tags, as the different terms extracted from different papers may refer
to the same thing. In addition, the reliability of the mapping stage was
essential to ensure that the interpretation of the data as objective and
consistent with the original meaning behind the extracted data. We
made several discussions and changes to the data presentation to ensure
that the final charts reflected the original intent of the extracted data.
9. Conclusion and future work
In this paper, we have conducted a systematic literature review on
ML4RE, integrating practitioners‘ insights from Stack Overflow. Toward
academia, we systematically searched and selected academic publica-
tions from four academic databases (IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library,
SpringerLink, and Scopus). We obtained 207 publications published
in ML4RE between 2010 and 2022. For grey literature, we systemat-
ically searched Stack Overflow and obtained 375 questions asked by
practitioners in RE practices from 2010 to 2022 with the answers to
these questions. We analyse the literature’s trends, RE activities, RE
tasks, solutions, and data. By analysing and integrating the extracted
information, we present the current status of ML4RE in academic
research and practices in Stack Overflow. Lastly, we compare the results
of these two parts to discern their similarities and differences. Based on
our research results, we discuss some interesting phenomena worthy of
attention in ML4RE and give some research suggestions.
Our future work could continue to investigate the latest research
trends in this field. In addition, more grey literature could be involved
to listen more fully to the voices of practitioners and the industry.
