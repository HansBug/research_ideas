During code search, queries are reformulated in multiple steps. These steps might vary based on
eithertypeofreformulationortypeofcodesearch.However,mostexistingapproaches[ 54,63,64,
155,156,183] shareacommon setof steps(Figure 3) asfollows:

query(seesteps2–4inFigure 3).Onewaytocollectfeedbackisthe relevancefeedbackmethod
[169],whichhasbeenwidelyusedintheIRdomain.Inthismethod,eachofthedocuments

2.4 Term Weighting
Term weighting is a popular method to determine the relative importance of individual terms
withinanybodyoftexts(e.g.,changerequest,sourcecode)[ 82].Itisoftenusedtoselectcandidate
keywordsduringautomatedqueryreformulations(seestep6inFigure 3).Severaltermweighting
algorithms had been proposed and widely used over the past few decades. They can be classified
into thefollowingthreebroadcategories:
(a)Frequency-based term weighting : The importance of a keyword is determined based on its
occurrencefrequencies.Thearguablymostpopularfrequency-basedmethodisTF-IDF[ 82],
whichstandsforTermFrequency(TF)timesInverseDocumentFrequency(IDF).TFcounts
the occurrences of a term (or keyword) within a document, whereas DF counts the number
ofdocuments(withinacorpus)containingtheterm.However,IDF,theinverseofdocument
frequency,determinestheinformativenessofatermbypenalizingthehighlyfrequentterms

(e.g., stop words). Thus, TF-IDF determines a term’s importance without considering the
dependenciesthatit might haveon otherterms.
(b)Graph-based term weighting : Unlike the frequency-based methods, graph-based methods
[21,156,158] capture the dependencies among keywords during their weight calculation.
Existingstudiescaptureco-occurrences[ 126],syntacticdependencies[ 21],andhierarchical
dependencies[ 158]amongthekeywords,andtransformatextdocumentintoagraphstruc-
ture. In this graph, the keywords are represented as nodes and their dependencies as edges.
Then the important nodes (or keywords) are identified using a graph-based, recursive al-
gorithm, namely PageRank [ 27]. Source code has an unrestricted, evolving vocabulary that
could be larger than that of regular texts [ 69]. Furthermore, the code is rich in terms of
structuresanddependencies(e.g.,staticrelations).Thus,graph-basedalgorithmsleveraging
dependenciescouldbeasuitablechoicetoselectimportantkeywordsfromthesourcecode
duringqueryreformulations[ 155,158].

methods make use of Information Theory to calculate the term weights [ 28,30,32]. One
of thepopularprobabilisticmethodsisKullback–LeiblerDivergence,whichdeterminesthe
divergence of a keyword (within a document) from the random occurrence probability. In
other words, if a keyword is frequent across numerous documents within a corpus (a.k.a. a
generickeyword),itdoesnotcontainuniqueinformation,whichmakesitlessimportant.On
thecontrary,akeywordthatisspecifictoonlyafewdocumentsoftencontainsmeaningful
information,whichmakes itimportant.
2.5 Implications of Automated Query Reformulation
Choosing the right queries during code search is a challenging problem. Hence, automated sup-
ports for constructing queries are highly warranted for developers. However, automatic reformu-
lationsof querieshavebothpositiveandnegative implications,asfollows:
(a)Benefits of query reformulation : Developers often use short queries to search for relevant
code, which might not always reflect their information needs [ 48]. Thus, expanding these
short queries with similar or complementary keywords often improves their performance.
According to existing literature [ 160],≈20% performance gain could be achieved in code
searchusingautomaticallyreformulatedqueries.Carefullyreformulatedqueriesmightalso
helpdeveloperslocatetheirdesiredcodewithreducedcognitive or manualefforts.
(b)Costsofqueryreformulation :Automaticreformulationsmighthurtthesearchqueriesthatare
alreadygood(orappropriate)[ 31,63,155].Addingextrakeywordsmakesthemnoisy[ 179].
Thus,noreformulationisbetterforthemthaninappropriatereformulations[ 140,179,184].
Therealsoexistafewdifficultqueriesthatcannotbeimprovedusingtraditionalreformula-
tionapproaches[ 31,57].
Given these twofold implications of query reformulations, contemporary approaches attempt
to maximize the benefits and minimize the costs of reformulation. For example, several studies
[63,64,155] adopt supervised machine learning and query quality analysis to improve the poor
queriesandtopreservethegoodqueriesduringthecodesearch.

3.5 GroundedTheoryBased Analysis
We use a Grounded Theory approach [ 58] to analyze our primary studies. It is widely used in
social science research to derive meaningful theories that are firmly grounded on the data (e.g.,
interview scripts). Recently, this approach has also found applications in Software Engineering
research [ 35,150,175]. We systematically analyze the full texts from each of the primary studies,

TheGroundedTheoryapproachinvolves thefollowing threestagesof coding.

5.2 Design Practical GA-BasedQuery Reformulations for IR-Based Bug Localization
Mills et al. [ 131] demonstrate that GAs are capable of generatingoptimal or near-optimal queries
from a bug report (that can localize faulty code) when the ground truth information is provided.
Unfortunately, in practice, the ground truth might not be known when a search query is con-
structed to find a bug. Thus, the ground truth should not be a part of fitness calculation when
designing a GA-based query reformulation technique. In other words, we need a fitness function
that can identify the better one from two different search queries without needing their ground
truth,whichis amajor challenge.
Future Work .Several studies [ 63,64,130,132,155] make use of query difficulty metrics (e.g.,
specificity, coherency [ 33]) and machine learning algorithms to predict a query’s success against
the search engines. However, these metrics have non-linear relationships with a query’s real per-
formance according to recent studies [ 130,151]. One possible way to address this is to formulate
the query construction as a multi-objective optimization problem, as demonstrated by Kim and
Lee [91]. They design a complex fitness function that attempts to optimize 15 objectives while
reformulating a bug report into an optimal query for IR-based bug localization. Future work can
designoneormorefitnessfunctionsthatcanbeusedinpracticeforGA-basedkeywordselection
from bug reports. For example, the difficulty of a query could be a part of its fitness calculation
wheretheeasy querieswillbeprioritizedover thedifficultonesduringevolutionarysearch.
